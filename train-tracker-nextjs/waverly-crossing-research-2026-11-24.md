# Advance train alerts for Waverly, Nebraska

Research date: November 24, 2026

**Can Train Tracker tell a driver leaving in ten minutes that a train is likely to delay their trip, without asking anyone to report it or installing hardware ourselves?**

For Amtrak, there is enough public data to prototype that alert now. For freight, the November 2026 review found commercial services that predict crossing delays, but could not verify a free public feed covering Waverly. The strongest lead is the Nebraska Department of Transportation (NDOT). Its active Omaha–Lincoln study covers the BNSF corridor, and university researchers have previously observed trains at one of Waverly’s crossings. [Amtraker documentation](https://github.com/piemadd/amtrak) · [TRAINFO prediction service](https://trainfo.ca/solutions/reduce-first-responder-delay/) · [NDOT corridor study](https://ndot-omaha-lincoln-rail.com/) · [UNL research site](https://matc.unl.edu/unl-resources/)

## The two crossings

| Road | U.S. DOT crossing ID | Estimated daily through trains, 2023 inventory counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N 141st Street | 074940T | 37, including 2 passenger trains. [Federal inventory form](https://safetydata.fra.dot.gov/OfficeofSafety/PublicSite/Crossing/Crossing.aspx?txtcrossingnum=074940T&phasetype=C&rpttype=I) |
| N 148th Street | 074942G | 38, including 2 passenger trains. [Federal inventory form](https://safetydata.fra.dot.gov/OfficeofSafety/PublicSite/Crossing/Crossing.aspx?phasetype=C&rpttype=I&txtcrossingnum=074942G) |

The crossings are about half a rail mile apart on BNSF’s Creston Subdivision. At a steady 45 mph, a train covers that distance in under a minute and travels 7.5 rail miles in ten minutes. A gate event at 141st could confirm a current delay or help validate a forecast. A ten-minute forecast needs an earlier observation on the correct approach, with enough time left after the data reaches the app. For a driver leaving in ten minutes, time from departure to the crossing also matters. The useful alert is whether the predicted blockage overlaps the driver's arrival window.

## What the app currently measures

The browser checks the reporter’s location before enabling the reporting controls. The API stores a crossing-or-clear report in PostgreSQL and broadcasts it through Pusher. The report record has no crossing ID, so both streets share one status. The server calculates consensus from recent reports, but the main screen displays the latest report. Its default ten-minute countdown measures report expiry. There is no upstream train observation or predicted clearance time in that calculation. ## First freight experiment

NDOT’s published schedule places the corridor study in its recommendations phase from October through December 2026. Its project contact is **connect@NDOT-Omaha-Lincoln-Rail.com**. The following is a draft inquiry. [Study schedule and contact](https://ndot-omaha-lincoln-rail.com/)

> Does NDOT, Waverly, UNL, or BNSF already collect timestamped train detections on either approach to Waverly, plus gate activation and clearance times at N 141st Street (074940T) or N 148th Street (074942G)? We’re exploring a free community app that warns drivers of likely delays. Could we evaluate a historical sample, and is there a live feed we could use to publish crossing-delay estimates?

If a sample is available, match upstream detections to actual gate-down and gate-up times by crossing and direction. Replay the events using the time each observation would have reached the app. Measure how many blockages received at least ten minutes of warning, how many alerts predicted a delay that never occurred, and how closely the predicted blockage windows matched the recorded ones. If only gate records are available, they can still establish how often each road was blocked and for how long. The Amtrak prototype can proceed independently.
